Google discounts lastmod per-site once it proves unreliable, and that discount would have landed on the 26 blog entries where the dates are real and useful. Hand-authored pages take theirs from src/_data/pageDates.json (git history), for the same reason: item.date is the file mtime, and Cloudflare Pages builds from a fresh clone, so live all 33 page URLs shared the checkout timestamp and moved forward on every deploy. It only looked correct locally, where a working tree has real mtimes. Blog posts are not in pageDates — they carry real front matter. An explicitly revised post sets `dateModified`, and that has to win over `date`: lastmod exists to say "this changed", so a substantially rewritten post reporting its original publication date is the one case where lastmod is actively useless.
