{%- comment -%} Cookie-consent bar, included on every page of both editions from base.html. `hidden` is the default and js/consent.js decides: it shows the bar only when localStorage holds no decision yet, and the footer's "Cookie settings" link brings it back afterwards. With JavaScript off the bar never appears — and neither does any analytics tag, because head.html parks those as `type="text/plain"` and only consent.js can turn them into real scripts. No consent, no measurement, in every case including the broken ones. TWO LAYERS, so granularity does not cost height. At rest this is one row: a sentence and three buttons. The per-purpose checkboxes live in a panel that opens only if someone asks for it — which is the pattern the EDPB's guidance actually requires: accept and reject equally easy at the top level, granular choice available rather than unavoidable. consent.js opens the panel by itself when the stored decision is a MIX, because that is the one case where the summary row cannot show what is already on. TWO PURPOSES, not one bundle. Counting page views is aggregate; recording clicks, scrolling and pointer movement is a recording of behaviour. Bundling them would make the consent for the intrusive half no more specific than for the harmless one. Each checkbox gates its own vendor through its own `data-consent` tag in head.html. Both start UNTICKED — a pre-ticked box is not consent. "Decline all" and "Allow all" are the same size and weight, because refusing has to be exactly as easy as agreeing. Copy is load-bearing: purpose, the fact nothing runs without a yes, the right to withdraw and where, and the no-sale line CCPA/CPRA needs. Controller identity, retention and transfers stay one click away in /privacy/ — that is what a layered notice is for.
